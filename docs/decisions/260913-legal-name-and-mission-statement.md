# 2026-09-13 — Adopt legal name and mission statement

**Status:** ACCEPTED
**Affects:** [`current/02-entity-structure.md`](../current/02-entity-structure.md), [`current/01-mission-and-scope.md`](../current/01-mission-and-scope.md) (new)
**Supersedes:** the "Exact legal name" open question in `current/02-entity-structure.md`; the mission-statement portion of [`drafts/mission-and-governance-pivot.md`](../drafts/mission-and-governance-pivot.md) (260719, status SKETCH)

## Context

Two open questions had been sitting unresolved since the 260509 formation decision: the entity's final legal name (deferred to counsel because "Trust" is restricted under Washington naming rules), and the mission statement (drafted as a SKETCH in the July 2026 mission-and-governance-pivot but never adopted).

We're now moving to incorporate, so both needed to be settled:

- **Name:** Avoid "Trust" entirely rather than lean on counsel to clear it. `02-entity-structure.md` had already flagged *Downtown Loyal Heights, Inc.* as a fallback for exactly this reason. A check of Washington's business registry and general web search turned up no conflicting or confusingly similar active entity (unrelated hits only: Loyal Heights Manor, Loyal Heights PTA).
- **Mission statement:** The pivot draft's proposed wording used "stewards" to describe the entity's role. A revision was floated swapping "stewards" for "organizes efforts to improve," which reads more like a generic civic-advocacy group and undersells the long-term custodial/governance role central to the whole DTLH concept (the entity is meant to eventually hold real estate and reserve major decisions, not just advocate). We kept "stewards."

## Decision

1. **Legal name:** ***Downtown Loyal Heights, Inc.***, a Washington nonprofit corporation under RCW 24.03A. No "Trust" in the legal name. "DTLH Trust" may still be used as a public/brand name if wanted, but it is not the entity of record.

2. **Mission statement:**

   > Downtown Loyal Heights stewards the Loyal Heights neighborhood of Seattle, ensuring that growth is shaped by, governed by, and benefits the people who live, work, and gather there for generations.

   This adopts the *wording* from the July 2026 pivot draft (broad benefit class — "live, work, and gather" rather than "residents"; not tied to the acquisition of one property), updated to match the new legal name, with "stewards" retained rather than softened to "organizes efforts to improve."

This decision adopts the **legal name and mission statement only**. It does **not** adopt the rest of the July 2026 pivot draft — the self-perpetuating board and the simplified single-LLC investment structure remain SKETCH, undecided, and open for separate decision records.

## Consequences

- `current/02-entity-structure.md`'s "Downtown Loyal Heights Trust, Inc. (working name)" references and its "Exact legal name" open question are resolved and should be updated to the adopted name.
- A new `current/01-mission-and-scope.md` is created (status AGREED) carrying this mission statement, filling the previously-placeholder slot in the docs index.
- The formal 501(c)(3)/(c)(4) purposes clause in the Articles is still a separate, counsel-drafted sentence — this mission statement is the public-facing one and does not need to match the Articles' legal purpose language word-for-word.
- The site copy (homepage, "Governance" section) and `docs/drafts/site-update-language.md` still reference the old "Downtown Loyal Heights Trust" framing and should be updated to the new name when the entity is filed.
- Governance structure (self-perpetuating board vs. member elections) and investment structure (single LLC vs. multi-class) remain open and unresolved by this record.

## Alternatives considered

**Keep "Trust" in the name and let counsel clear it.** Rejected. Washington's restriction on "Trust" in entity names is a real friction point with no clear upside to fighting it; a clean name avoids a filing delay for no real benefit, and "DTLH Trust" is preserved as a brand.

**"Organizes efforts to improve" instead of "stewards."** Rejected. It reads as advocacy-only and undersells the ownership/governance role the entity is meant to eventually play.
