# We live in one of the most affluent societies ever created.

If the Seattle metro were a country, its output per person would rank near the top of the world — above Switzerland, Singapore, and Norway. By the long-run historical record, no society before us comes close.

# 80th & 24th is for sale

- **Three adjacent parcels** at the southeast corner of NW 80th St and 24th Ave NW — a combined **42,434 sf** — are listed for **$9.5M** ($224/sf) as a development site

- Whatever gets built there will reshape our neighborhood for the next several decades

> In determining which set of an organization’s patrons should own the firm, there are two basic considerations. The first is: which group of patrons is most subject to exploitation by the firm? The second is: which group of patrons can govern the firm most effectively?

<div class="eyebrow">The idea</div>
<hr class="deck-rule">

# A neighborhood-owned corner

Form a community-governed firm entity to:

- **Invest in** or **acquire** parcels
- **Design and build** something the neighborhood actually wants
- **Hold** the property as a long-term, neighborhood-owned asset

Equity holders share in appreciation and rental income. Community governance lets equity holders influence what gets built.

---

<div class="eyebrow">Why now</div>
<hr class="deck-rule">

# Growth is coming either way

- **2019** — Seattle City Council votes unanimously to upzone 27 neighborhoods, including Crown Hill and greater Ballard
- **2024** — The One Seattle Plan designates Ballard a **"Regional Center"**, the city's highest density category, alongside Downtown and Capitol Hill
- **2026** — Ballard's Q1 home sales are the highest since 2022; buyers compete with town-home developers and all-cash investors

This is exactly the kind of parcel that normally gets bought and built without the neighborhood having any say. **We have a narrow window to do it differently.**

<div class="eyebrow">Structure</div>
<hr class="deck-rule">

# Hybrid non-profit for-profit structure

**A non-profit.** Founded with the mission to make Loyal Heights a clean and safe place to raise kids and grow old. Can receive charitable donations.

**A for-profit.** Leadership team is selected by the board on the non-profit. Has the ability to raise capital to invest in property. 

---

<div class="eyebrow">Participation</div>
<hr class="deck-rule">

# Three tiers

| Tier | Who | What they get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Anchor investors** — ~5 at ~$1M each | Accredited investors | Preferred return, board seats, development upside |
| **Neighbor investors** — $10K and up | Loyal Heights residents | Pro-rata equity, a voice in governance, a real stake in the corner |
| **Smaller investors** | More residents, once we're on our feet | Lower-dollar buy-in paths (e.g. monthly community-investment-trust model) |

<div class="eyebrow">The insight</div>
<hr class="deck-rule">

# The fewer the merrier

Why did London's profit-seeking developers build hundreds of garden squares? Under **unified land ownership**, the owner captures the value a public good adds to every surrounding property — parks, street trees, and beautiful facades become *profitable*. Fragmented ownership kills that math. And where unified ownership survives, it still works:

- **Clovelly, Devon** — one family, car-free since forever, every property maintained
- **De Beauvoir Town** — the Benyon Estate buys and refurbishes blighted properties *because it owns their neighbors*
- **Sloane Street** — the Cadogan Estate just spent **£50M** of its own money widening pavements and planting 100 trees on a street it doesn't even own

<p class="tagline">— Samuel Hughes, <a href="https://www.worksinprogress.news/p/the-fewer-the-merrier">"The fewer the merrier"</a>, Works in Progress</p>

---

<div class="eyebrow">Governance</div>
<hr class="deck-rule">

# A great estate, run democratically

A single coordinating owner made those neighborhoods work. DTLH recreates that effect — accountable to the neighborhood rather than an English duke.

- **The Trust** (nonprofit) — run by neighbors, who elect its board; holds the long view
- **The Company** — owns the real estate; its board runs financing, leases, tenants; anchors who want a seat have one
- **Two share classes** — economic-only shares open to anyone; voting shares only for Loyal Heights residents

**Anyone can invest. Only residents (within some kind of radius) vote.**
